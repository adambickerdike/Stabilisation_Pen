# Interview guide B: occupational therapists, Parkinson's nurses and related clinicians (EXP-U03)

**Purpose.** Learn how clinicians meet writing problems in ET, PD and other tremor today, what they recommend, how they measure writing, who pays for equipment, and what evidence would make them recommend or buy a new tool (a measuring pen, a tablet app, a therapy kit, a desk surface or an active pen).

**Who.** 12–16 clinicians (saturation guidance, LITERATURE, HAP-164): occupational therapists (adult neurology, community equipment, paediatric for guide C), Parkinson's disease nurse specialists, movement-disorder neurologists or trial investigators, and speech and language therapists (for the dictation question). Mix NHS and private practice, and at least two people who run or recruit to clinical trials.

**Recruitment.** Professional networks and charities' professional channels (see `recruitment_and_ethics.md`). If clinicians are approached as NHS staff at NHS sites for a research study, HRA and HCRW Approval may apply (PDT-119); check with the sponsor's research office before contact.

**Length.** 40–45 minutes. No patient-identifiable information is collected; ask for anonymised examples only.

---

## 1. Opening script

"We are researching everyday writing difficulties in tremor and Parkinson's, and how clinicians support them. We are not selling anything today. Please do not share anything that could identify a patient. You can skip any question."

## 2. Caseload (5 min)

1. Roughly how many people with ET, PD or other tremor do you see in a typical month? How many raise writing?
2. Which writing problems come up most (tremor, small writing, slowness, fatigue, signing)? Which matter most to patients?
3. At what point do patients usually raise writing: early, when it affects work, when a document must be signed?

## 3. How writing is assessed now (8 min)

1. What do you use to assess writing? (Probe: a handwriting sample, spirals, TETRAS or Fahn-Tolosa-Marín items, the Bain-Findley ADL writing item, the MDS-UPDRS 2.7 patient item, the SOS test, timed copying; wearable monitors.)
2. How long does it take? What would you do differently if it took 5 minutes and gave numbers you trust?
3. Is writing measured in any trial you work on? Which endpoint? Who decides the endpoint?
4. What would a measurement tool need before you would use it: agreement with a clinical scale, test-retest reliability, time, cost, data security, regulatory marking?

## 4. What you recommend today (8 min)

For each option, ask what they recommend, for whom, and what happens in practice:
- weighted or thick pens and grips; clipboards and non-slip mats;
- practice, lined paper, cueing (for PD small writing);
- typing on phones or computers, and accessibility settings;
- dictation (probe: when does it fail? soft or unclear speech?);
- gloves, wrist weights, stabilising utensils;
- medicines or surgery (context only).

Then: "What do people come back and tell you did not work?"

## 5. Provision and who pays (7 min)

1. How do patients get equipment: NHS community equipment services, a charity, buying it themselves, their employer (Access to Work), insurance?
2. Is there a budget or a formulary for writing aids? Who signs it off?
3. Do you know of VAT relief on disability aids (HMRC Notice 701/7, PDT-117)? Do patients use it?
4. What price would stop you recommending a device that works? What price would need a funding application?

## 6. Evidence you would need (7 min)

Show the four concept cards (same wording as guide A, including the caveat that nothing is available or proven).

1. For each: who on your caseload, if anyone, would it suit? Who would it not suit?
2. What evidence would you need before recommending it? (Probe: comparison with a weighted pen and with typing or dictation; a blinded reading of the writing; safety; results for each condition separately.)
3. Any safety concerns? (Probe: magnets and implanted devices such as deep brain stimulators or pacemakers; heat; falls; fatigue.)
4. For the measuring pen: would you use it in clinic, at home between visits, or only in trials? How often?

## 7. Willingness to take part (3 min)

1. Would your service consider hosting a small study (EXP-U06 or EXP-U08)?
2. Could you share a study invitation with patients (with ethics approval)?

## 8. Close

Thank the person; explain the honorarium (if any) and its terms. Ask whether they may be recontacted.

## Coding frame

| Code family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Assessment | scale used, time, trust, gaps |
| Recommendation | aid, practice, typing, dictation, none |
| Failure reasons | fatigue, weight, speech, cognition, cost, adherence |
| Provision route | NHS equipment service, charity, self-pay, employer, insurer |
| Evidence bar | comparator, blinding, outcome, sample size |
| Safety flags | implants, heat, falls |
| Measuring use | clinic, home, trials, frequency, price anchor |
