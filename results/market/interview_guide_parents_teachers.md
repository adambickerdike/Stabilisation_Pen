# Interview guide C: parents, teachers, SENCOs and school occupational therapists (EXP-U04)

**Purpose.** Learn how much handwriting children with writing difficulties (developmental coordination disorder, dysgraphia, dyslexia) must still do, what support they get, what fails, and who would buy a tablet app, a therapy kit or a writing aid.

**Who.** Adults only: 12–16 interviews (HAP-164) split across parents or carers, class teachers, special educational needs coordinators (SENCOs) and school or paediatric occupational therapists, in primary and secondary schools. Children are not interviewed in this study. Any later study that involves children needs verified permission from a responsible adult, an age check before other personal data, and the child's own chance to decline, including in school (MRS Code rules 16–22, PDT-119), plus the study's safeguarding checks.

**Length.** 30–40 minutes.

**Do not.** Collect the child's name, school or work samples. Ask for descriptions, not documents. Do not suggest that any product treats dyslexia (the project's rule, HAP-47).

---

## 1. Opening script

"We are researching how children with handwriting difficulties manage writing at school and at home. We are not selling anything, and we will not ask for your child's or pupils' names. You can skip any question."

## 2. The writing load (8 min)

1. In a normal school day, how much does the child (or a typical pupil you support) write by hand? Which lessons? Homework?
2. What happens in tests and exams? (Probe: extra time, a scribe, speech recognition, a word processor. In England the normal way of working in exams is handwriting unless an arrangement applies; HAP-153.)
3. Tell me about the last time writing went badly for the child. What happened?

## 3. Support today (8 min)

1. What support does the child get: occupational therapy, handwriting groups, pencil grips, sloped boards, lined paper, typing, apps?
2. How often and for how long? (Probe: effective programmes include at least 20 practice sessions; HAP-41.)
3. What helped? What did not? Why was anything stopped?
4. Has typing been offered instead? How did that go? Does the child still have to handwrite?

## 4. Technology at school and home (6 min)

1. Which devices does the child use: tablet, stylus, laptop, phone? Owned by school or family?
2. Any rules or worries about screen time, data or apps in school?
3. Who chooses and pays for apps or equipment: parents, the class, the school's SEN budget, the local authority, an OT service?

## 5. Concept reactions (8 min)

Show only the cards relevant to children, with the caveat "an idea being studied; not available; not proven":

- **Card B. A tablet app with a stylus** for letter-formation practice and measuring progress, used with the teacher or therapist.
- **Card C. A practice kit with the therapist**: short daily exercises with feedback on paper or tablet.
- **Card A (optional). A pen that steadies itself** (show only if the adult raises tremor or motor control).

For each:
1. Would this fit the child's day? When exactly?
2. What would make you stop using it after a week?
3. What would you need to see before paying or asking the school to pay (evidence, trial period, therapist recommendation)?
4. Price: what would feel like a bargain, expensive but acceptable, too expensive (per year for an app, one-off for a kit)?

## 6. Close

Ask whether the adult would answer a short follow-up survey (EXP-U04 survey) and whether their school or service might pilot an app (GNG-9). Explain any thank-you payment and its terms.

---

## Coding frame

| Code family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Writing load | daily minutes, subjects, homework, exams |
| Arrangements | extra time, scribe, word processor, speech recognition |
| Support | OT, groups, grips, boards, apps, typing |
| Failure reasons | boredom, stigma, time, no transfer to schoolwork |
| Buyer | parent, school SEN budget, local authority, OT service |
| Unmet job | a school writing task current support does not solve (feeds GNG-9) |
