# Interview guide A: adults with essential tremor or Parkinson's (EXP-U02)

**Purpose.** Learn which writing jobs people still do, which they have given up, what they use instead, and what they would pay to get back. The interview is about the person's life, not about our product. The product concepts appear only in section 6.

**Who.** Adults (18+) with clinician-diagnosed essential tremor (ET) or Parkinson's (PD) who say writing is affected (screening survey item S7 ≥ 1). Plan 12–16 interviews per diagnosis. Empirical studies reach saturation within 9–17 interviews for homogeneous groups (LITERATURE, HAP-164). Stratify each diagnosis by age (under 65 / 65+) and by self-rated severity (S7 = 1–2 / 3–4). Include at least 3 left-handed people and at least 3 people of working age.

**Stop rule (set in advance).** After 12 interviews in a diagnosis group, stop when 3 interviews in a row add no new job code (ASSUMPTION).

**Length.** 45 minutes, 60 at most. Offer a break every 15 minutes. Offer two shorter sessions if the person tires.

**Mode.** In person, video or phone. Offer large print, extra time, a carer or family member present (the participant answers), and typed or chat answers for anyone whose speech is hard to hear.

**Do not.**
- Collect or photograph a real signature, a real form or a real cheque. If the person wants to show how they write, use a made-up name and a made-up sentence (pseudo-signature, as in `validation/human_study_plan.md` §10).
- Diagnose, rate severity out loud, or give medical advice. If the person is distressed or reports a new symptom, use the distress protocol at the end.
- Pitch, or say that the device works. Nothing has been built and nothing is proven.

---

## 0. Before the interview

- Check the signed consent form and the optional recording consent.
- Note the screening answers (diagnosis, age band, dominant hand, S7, devices owned) so you do not ask again.
- Have ready: the four concept cards (section 6), the price sheet (section 7), a pen, plain and lined paper.

## 1. Opening script (read as written)

"Thank you for talking with us. We are a research team studying how people manage everyday writing: things like lists, cards, forms and signatures, on paper or on screens. We are not selling anything today, and there are no right or wrong answers. The most useful thing you can tell us is what really happens, including the times you avoid writing or ask someone else. You can skip any question and stop at any time. Is it all right to start?"

## 2. Warm-up (5 min)

1. Tell me a little about a normal week for you. Where does writing or typing come into it?
2. When did you first notice that writing had become harder? What changed?

## 3. Last-time stories (15 min)

Ask for a specific recent occasion each time. Use "the last time", not "usually". Probe: what, where, sitting or standing, how long, who read it, how it went, what you did afterwards.

1. **Lists and notes.** Tell me about the last time you wrote a shopping list, a note or a reminder by hand.
2. **Signature.** Tell me about the last time you had to sign something on paper: a form, a delivery, a card, a bank or legal document, a postal vote.
   - Probe: did anyone question the signature? Did you have to sign again? Did someone sign for you?
3. **Forms.** Tell me about the last paper form you filled in: at a doctor, a bank, a council, a school.
4. **Cards and letters.** The last birthday or greetings card you wrote.
5. **Avoided or delegated.** Tell me about the last time you chose not to write something, or asked someone else to write or sign for you. What was it? How did that feel?
6. **Drawing, puzzles, hobbies.** Is there anything you used to write or draw for pleasure that you have stopped?

## 4. Jobs, importance and frequency (8 min)

For each task mentioned, fill the grid together (read the scale aloud):

| Task | How often in the last month (every day / few times a week / few times a month / less / never) | How important that it is on paper and in your hand (1–5) | How hard now (1 none – 5 cannot) | What you do instead | What happens if it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Then ask:
1. Which one of these would you most like to be able to do again, or do more easily? Why that one?
2. For that task, could it be typed, dictated or done by someone else? What stops you?

## 5. Alternatives already tried (8 min)

1. **Typing.** Tell me about the last time you typed a message or a note on a phone, tablet or computer. How did it go? Have you changed any settings (for example, touch settings, larger keys, filters)?
2. **Voice.** Have you tried speaking to your phone or computer to write? The last time you did: how well did it understand you (0–10)? Where would you not use it (public, noisy places, private things)?
3. **Aids.** Which of these have you tried: weighted or thick pens, grips, a pen on a clipboard, gloves or wrist weights, a stabilising spoon, anything else? For each: how long did you use it, what was good, what made you stop? Score 0–10.
4. **Timing and medicine.** Do you plan writing around the time of day or medicine? (Record, do not advise.)
5. **Help from others.** Who helps you write or sign? How do you feel about that?

## 6. Concept reactions (7 min)

Show the four cards one at a time, in a random order (rotate across interviews). Read the caveat on each card: "This is an idea being studied. It is not available and has not been shown to help anyone."

- **Card A. A pen that steadies itself.** A pen about the size of a thick marker that moves its tip slightly to cancel small shakes. It needs charging.
- **Card B. A tablet app with a stylus.** Writing and drawing on a tablet screen with a pen; the app can show your writing larger and neater on screen, give practice exercises, and measure changes over time.
- **Card C. A practice kit with your therapist.** Short daily writing exercises with feedback, set up with an occupational therapist or nurse.
- **Card D. A desk writing surface.** A board that sits on a table and helps guide or steady the pen while you write on normal paper.

For each card ask:
1. What, if anything, would you use this for? Which task from section 4?
2. What worries you about it? (Probe: size and weight, charging, looks, noise, setting up, cost, being seen using it.)
3. How much time would you accept to set it up before writing: seconds, a minute, longer?
4. Would you rather have this, a better ordinary pen, or better typing or voice? Why?

## 7. Price and who pays (5 min)

Only for the card the person ranked most useful. Do not give a price first.

Van Westendorp questions (write the four numbers down):
1. At what price would it be so cheap that you would doubt it works?
2. At what price would it be a bargain?
3. At what price would it start to feel expensive, but you would still consider it?
4. At what price would it be too expensive to consider?

Then:
5. Who would pay: you, your family, the NHS or insurer, your employer, a charity? Have you ever received equipment through any of these?
6. Would you want to try it at home for a month before paying?

## 8. Close (2 min)

1. Is there anything about writing that we did not ask but should have?
2. Would you like to hear about future research (a separate yes or no on the consent form)?
3. Thank the person. Explain the thank-you payment: who pays it, what it is, when it arrives and any condition (MRS Code rules 25–26, PDT-119). Do not offer the project's own products as a thank-you.

## Distress and safety protocol

- If the person becomes upset: pause, offer a break or to stop, and do not continue the topic unless they want to.
- If they describe a new or worsening symptom, a fall, or thoughts of self-harm: stop the interview, follow the study's safeguarding procedure, and suggest they contact their GP, specialist nurse or an emergency service as appropriate. Give the Parkinson's UK or tremor charity helpline details listed in the participant information sheet.
- Record the event in the study log. Do not record clinical details in the interview notes.

## Coding frame (for analysis)

| Code family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Job | list, note, signature (legal, delivery, card), form (health, bank, council, school), card or letter, cheque, puzzle, drawing, work document |
| Setting | seated at a table, standing, in public, at home |
| Pain | illegible, slow, tiring, embarrassing, rejected signature, needs a helper, avoided |
| Workaround | typing, dictation, helper signs or writes, pre-printed labels, avoiding the task, medicine timing |
| Aid history | tried, abandoned (reason), kept |
| Unserved job | a paper-bound task that typing, dictation and current aids do not solve (feeds GNG-2) |
| Adoption barrier | weight, charging, looks, setup time, cost, trust |
| Payer | self, family, NHS, insurer, employer (Access to Work), charity |

Two coders code the first four transcripts independently and agree the codebook before coding the rest.
