# Recruitment routes and ethics note (EXP-U01 to EXP-U09)

This note covers the customer-discovery work (survey, interviews, waitlist) and the user comparison studies. It is planning advice, not an ethics approval. The sponsor's research office, a regulatory adviser and a data protection officer must confirm each point.

## 1. Recruitment routes

| Route | Who it reaches | What it requires (as found) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parkinson's UK Take Part Hub and Research Support Network | People with Parkinson's and carers in the UK | "your full ethical approval or support letter" and a participant information sheet; shared "for free"; allow up to 10 weeks; first reply within 5 working days (PDT-119). Commercial eligibility is set by its Research Support Policy (not read) | Verified page; policy to read |
| International Essential Tremor Foundation (US) | People with ET; a 2021 survey was emailed to 19 206 people on its database and 2864 answered (PDT-99) | Terms not found; contact the foundation | To contact |
| National Tremor Foundation (UK tremor charity) | People with ET and other tremor in England and Wales, local support groups | Not reviewed | To contact |
| Parkinson's Foundation, Michael J. Fox Foundation (US) | People with PD; Fox Insight is an online research cohort (8536 early-PD respondents in PDT-102) | Not reviewed | To contact |
| PD nurse specialists, movement-disorder clinics, community OT services | Patients in care; clinicians for guide B | If the research takes place in the NHS in England or Wales and the NHS has a duty of care to participants (patients or staff), HRA and HCRW Approval applies (PDT-119) | Needs HRA route if used |
| Professional bodies and networks for occupational therapists and PD nurses | Clinicians | Not reviewed | To contact |
| Dyspraxia and dyslexia charities, handwriting associations, school networks | Parents, teachers, SENCOs | Not reviewed | To contact |
| Paid online panels and adverts | Wider reach, less engaged people | Platform health-advertising policies not reviewed | Optional |

**Bias warning.** Charity members are more engaged and often more severely affected than the whole population: in one community study half of the people with ET did not notice their tremor and 92 % had never been prescribed treatment for it (PDT-97). Report the recruitment route for every answer and never present charity-sample percentages as population figures.

## 3. Consent and capacity

- Written or recorded consent before any data collection; plain language; large print; extra time; a carer may help but the participant decides.
- Separate, optional permissions: recording; recontact; waitlist; reuse of anonymised data.
- Capacity: exclude only people who cannot consent; cognitive impairment is recorded, not a reason to exclude by itself (as in `validation/human_study_plan.md` §3.2).
- Speech difficulties: offer typed or chat answers; never exclude someone from the dictation question because their speech is unclear. That is the question.

## 4. Children

The discovery work interviews adults only. Any study with children needs verified permission from a responsible adult, an age check before other personal data, a child-appropriate information sheet, and the child's own right to decline, including in school (MRS Code rules 16–22, PDT-119). Add the study's safeguarding and criminal-record checks for staff. Never suggest that a writing aid treats dyslexia (HAP-47).

## 5. Vulnerability and fairness

- Assess and meet participants' needs; no pressure to take part; an easy way to decline (MRS Code rules 23–24).
- Incentives: state who pays, what, when and on what condition; do not use the project's own products or vouchers for them as research incentives (MRS Code rules 25–26).
- No money or deposits from patients during discovery (DEC-094, proposed).

## 6. Data protection

- Health information is special-category personal data under UK GDPR and GDPR: carry out a data protection impact assessment before the first study (as `validation/human_study_plan.md` §10 already requires).
- Handwriting and signatures can identify people: never collect real signatures; use a pseudo-signature (a practised made-up name) in all tasks.
- Keep contact details apart from answers; pseudonymise; state the retention period; honour withdrawal.

## 7. Honest communication

- Every participant-facing text says the ideas are in development and unproven.
- Landing pages follow CAP Code 12 (no health claims without evidence and conformity marking) (PDT-119).
- Report every result, including a "no-go".
