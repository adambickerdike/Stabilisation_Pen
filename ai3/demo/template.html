Spelling Pen Prototype

Write one word at a time in the pad. The pen reads your letters as you write, notices a likely misspelling, and shows here the physical cue the pen would give. It offers a few completions when you pause. Nothing on paper is ever changed: corrections live in the transcript, and the pen writes only what you accept.

Example page: "we went to the libary", written with real letters from the UJI data set (not your writing). Start your own page to write.

Pen: writing normally Write between the guide lines; lift the pen between letters. Leave a wide gap, or press Next word.

    How this works, and what the study found

    Letters: a small recurrent network that reads the pen path point by point (task 1 of study S), trained on UJI Pen Characters v2 (Llorens et al., CC BY 4.0), running in this page. Spelling: a noisy-channel model; score = λ·log P(strokes | letters) + log P(word) + log P(letters | word), with the error model estimated from real misspellings (Birkbeck corpus; only aggregate edit statistics are included) and a 20 000-word lexicon counted from CC0 sentences (Tatoeba, Common Voice). The combined probability is calibrated by temperature scaling. Names and words you mark as intended are never flagged. The pen cue, completions and writing plan are a proposed design shown in simulation; nothing here was measured on people or on a pen.
