Stabilisation Pen

An actively stabilising pen for essential tremor, Parkinson's, poor handwriting and dyslexia, with AI note capture. Everything here is design, calculation and simulation. Nothing has been built or tested on people yet.

Where things stand (30 September 2026)

New engineering improvement report: implemented code, proposed CAD, corrected simulations and the remaining physical limits.

  • A more explicit nib design: a conditional 24 mm body / 1.5 mm travel candidate and a separately resized 20 mm body / 1.059 mm travel candidate. Force, guide friction and heat constrain both; neither is a complete manufactured pen.
  • A concrete route to accepted letters: a desk-supported five-bar plus fine nib completes the ink/tracking gate for all 20 tested suffixes without grip resistance, but none under either tested resisting grip. None of the unloaded traces also meets the selected actual acceleration/jerk limits. This is simulated mechanism performance, not patient benefit.
  • More realistic control: causal Hall and contact sensing, repaired gains, explicit missing/extra ink, and frozen comparisons. Six trained RL policies were rejected by their performance rules.
  • Reviewable spelling support: the offline application proposes corrections and requires selected acceptance while preserving original ink and recognition. Physical completion has separate reach, tracking and contact checks.

The studies use different physical models. Their favorable numbers cannot be combined into a specification for one validated device. Read the updated claims register before quoting results.

Round 5 (lead, 30 September 2026; decisions DEC-070 to DEC-099):

  • Reruns of the earlier headline simulations with realistic sensing (study X). Until then, those whole-pen simulation results count as upper bounds.
  • Done: the nib optimised (study N).
    • With two magnet sets, a ±1.5 mm nib fits the 24 mm pen within its heat limits: about 0.04 W in normal writing, 0.11 W at worst, skin 34 °C, about 23 hours per charge.
    • That is the reach study F found severe tremor needs, with perfect tremor knowledge.
    • The price is length: 157 mm against Rev K's 145 mm.
    • ±2 mm would need a body about 28 mm across.
    • The next step is to wind one and measure its force (EXP-NB01).
  • Done: a moving-paper writing surface (study P), a desk platen that moves the paper under the pen.
    • Reach is no longer the limit: with perfect knowledge of the shake it leaves 0.02 mm of 1.7 mm, and about 8 of 10 words are read, against about 4 for the ±1.5 mm nib.
    • Knowing the shake still decides: with today's best real tracker it leaves about 1 mm and under 1.5 words are read. A camera watching the pen tip would be good enough, once the shake can be told apart from the writing.
    • It writes accepted words by moving the page: 20 of 20 and 19 of 19 with the pen resting in a cradle; with a held pen only for a firm, calm hand.
    • The biggest unknown: how much the moving page drags a real hand's pen through the ball (EXP-PL03).
    • It is a desk device (380 × 300 mm, 4–5.5 kg, parts about USD 900–2,200). All of this is simulation; nothing is built.
  • Done: users and market (study U).
    • People still write by hand: 62 % of over-65s in Great Britain write lists by hand at least weekly, and some documents must be signed by hand.
    • Tremor affects typing too, and voice recognition struggles with Parkinson's speech.
    • But no one has yet shown a benefit from the active pen, and cheap aids compete. So the plan is:
      • ask first: a survey and interviews, with go/no-go numbers set in advance;
      • test the alternatives with people, with no new device;
      • offer measurement and practice products before the active pen.
  • Done: the first bench build plan (study H).
    • First build, a data box plus the contact-and-ink rig: about USD 4,350–7,750 and 4 weeks.
    • All five builds: about USD 14,000–35,400 over 12 weeks.
    • Parts lists with prices, safety rules and blank data sheets are included.
    • The calculated sideways load on the nib (about 50 mN) favours the 1.06 mm nib over the 1.5 mm one, unless measurements show less.

Earlier upstream summary, preserved for context

The statements below describe the original studies before the new sensor, control, dynamics and mechanism corrections. The improvement report states which results were rerun and which remain historical.

  • The moving tip can do the job if it knows the shake. With real recorded Parkinson's and essential tremor at the severe size, knowing the shake exactly would make about 7 of 10 words readable, against about 0.5 of 10 with an ordinary pen. simulation
  • Knowing the shake in time is the open problem. On real recorded tremor, no tracker built so far makes more words readable. For a clear gain at the severe size, a tracker must leave less than about 0.55 mm of the 1.7 mm shake at the tip. The best one leaves 1.1 mm. Predicting the shake is not the hard part: telling the shake apart from the writing is. Next: a small network trained on real recordings, tested once on new recordings (EXP-E10). simulation
  • The next prototype's nib is too short for severe tremor. With ±1 mm of reach, even perfect knowledge of the shake gains only about 1.4 readable words in 10 at the severe size. That would take about ±1.5 mm with perfect knowledge, or ±2–3 mm with a real tracker. The first Rev K pen is for mild to moderate tremor and for proving the new nib. simulation
  • The power problem has a designed fix. The balanced nib cancels the paper's sideways push, so the whole nib needs about 0.02–0.04 W instead of 1.6 W. Laid out as a complete pen (Rev K: 145 mm long, 66 g), it would write about 24–49 hours per charge. The price is reach: about ±1 mm, so the pen can no longer write whole words for you. Laying out the pen also left 9 of 59 fit checks failing. One example: the page sensor loses the page when the pen is lifted more than 0.2 mm. calculation
  • Moving the whole pen does not help. A collar that swings the whole inner pen ±4 mm changed the shake by −5 % to +5 %, against the 10 % a real gain would need. Tail weights were rejected too. simulation
  • The spelling help works in the app. The spell checker catches 63 % of real children's misspellings when it knows the letters, and 34 % when the pen reads them itself. After one letter, the right word is in the top 3 suggestions 52–56 % of the time. simulation and calculation
  • What to build first: the measurement rig R9, then the nib's side-load test (EXP-J17). See measurement rigs and prototype stages.

  • Shifting the whole pen: the collar and the tail weights, and why neither is carried forward.
