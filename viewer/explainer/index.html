Active pen · Rev J · proposed design

How the pen works

A pen for people whose hand shakes, whose writing has become small, or who find writing hard. You hold the outside of the pen, as with any pen. Inside it, three parts move to help your ink land where you meant it.

It has not been built yet. Everything below is a design and computer simulations, and every number says where it comes from.

Three parts that move

One sentence each: what moves, what pushes it, and what it does to your ink. The colours are the same in every picture on this page.

1The inner pen at the tip

The whole inner pen (the refill, its carrier and the arm, back to a pivot 76.5 mm behind the ball) swings inside the handle you hold, pushed by coils on the magnets at its back end, so the ball moves up to 6 mm against the shake and the ink stays on your letters.

steadies the inkwrites for you, if you turn it on

Being redesigned: holding the ball against the paper costs this design too much power (see Known problems).

Proposed designCalculation

2The heel wheel under the front ring

A 2 mm wheel under the front ring grips the paper and is steered, or driven, by two tiny motors, so the paper pushes the whole pen and your hand along the path of the letter, gently: at most 0.3–0.6 N, about the weight of an egg.

keeps your hand on the letterleads your hand, if you turn it on

Retracted by default: it is lowered onto the paper only when you turn guidance on (see Known problems).

3The tail weight in the end-cap you can take off

A 17 g tungsten weight in the end-cap is pushed from side to side by coils, and its push-back steadies the whole pen: a further 6–18 % less shake in the ink, but far too weak to move letters.

calms a fast shakeoptional

Optional: in the same simulation a plain weight of the same mass, locked in place, did better for one of the three grips, so the moving weight stays optional until it beats a locked one.

        How sure are we?

        Not sure yet. Every result on this page comes from computer simulations with made-up writers and made-up shakes. Nothing has been built, and nothing has been tested on a prototype or with people. Simulation only

        Known problems (being fixed)

        The whole-pen physics simulation and an independent review (29 September 2026) found these. They change the design, so the numbers they affect are marked “suspended” in the Details.

        1. The inner pen's coils spend most of their power just holding the ball against the paper. Because the pen is tilted, the paper pushes the ball sideways, and the magnets sit on a short arm, so they must push about 7 times harder. That is 1.6 W at a normal 50° angle and 4.7 W at 35°. The coils would overheat within about a minute. A balanced nib is being designed, so battery and heat figures are suspended.

          CalculationSimulation · sim2, the same load in a writing run

        2. The heel wheel moved clean writing by about 0.4 mm in the physics simulation, so it stays retracted unless the writer turns guidance on.

          Simulation · sim2

        3. The page sensor was assumed accurate to 3 µm (thousandths of a millimetre). A published research pen (DeltaPen, 2022) measured about 24–68 µm, so real accuracy is unproven.

          Assumption · the page sensorLiterature · OPT-02

        4. All writers and shakes so far are made up. Real recordings are being brought in.

          Simulation · synthetic writers and shakes

        Details

        Everything else is here, closed until you open it: the physics, every part, the modes, every result with its numbers, the simulation replays, the limits and what must be tested first. The inner pen is called the “nose” or “moving nose” in the design documents and in some of the tables.

        How each part pushes: the physicsA pen can push only against a weight inside it, your hand, or the paper. Why the paper gives the strong push, and why a weight cannot write.

        1Inner pen: pushes against your hand

        The coils push the inner pen one way and the handle the other way. Your hand is far heavier than the inner pen, so the ball moves and your hand hardly does. It is fast and precise, up to 6.0 mm. It cannot move your hand.

        Speed and precision, not force.

        Proposed designCalculation

        2Heel wheel: pushes against the paper

        The paper is always under the pen while you write, pressed by about 1 N. Literature · CON-01 A wheel that grips it can push with friction × load: about 0.3–0.6 N. A relaxed hand moves about 1.7 mm under 0.5 N. Lift the pen and the push is gone, so you can always get away.

        The strong push, from inside the pen.

        CalculationAssumption: friction, to measure (EXP-D01)

        3Tail weight: pushes against a weight

        Push a weight one way and the pen goes the other way. The push grows with the square of the speed: tiny at writing speed, strong at tremor speed. So it can calm a shake and give a short cue. It cannot write: at writing speeds the 45 g end-cap moves the ink at most 0.21 mm, and even a gyroscope only 1.06 mm, while half a letter is 2 mm.

        Calming and cues, never writing.

        CalculationSimulation · model H1
        So Rev J uses the paper for force, the inner pen for speed and precision, and the weight for calming and cues.

        The pen never takes over without your say. Steadying and steer-only guidance cannot start a stroke. Autowrite and lead-through are modes you turn on; they follow your sweep, wait when you slow, and a writer who resists always wins. Proposed design · DEC-037, DEC-039

        The parts, one by oneThe three places the pen acts, every component from the tip to the back, size, weight, battery and warmth, and which technologies are needed.

        1Tip the inner pen (moving nose)

        ±6.0 mm guaranteed at 35–75°; 6.57 mm at 50° (Rev H: 2.75 mm)

        The whole inner pen (the refill, its carrier and the arm) swings on a pivot 76.5 mm behind the ball, so the ball moves in every direction. It cancels tremor up to about 2 mm, and in autowrite it draws whole 2.5 mm letters of a known text while you sweep the pen along the line.

        CalculationSimulation · model HW1

        2Heel the wheel that grips the paper

        0.3–0.6 N of push on the hand

        A 2 mm wheel with a rubber tyre sits in a slot under the front ring. It uses the paper as ground. It is retracted by default: in the physics simulation it moved clean writing by about 0.4 mm. When you turn guidance on, it steers: it keeps your hand on the letter's path and cannot move the pen by itself. In a mode you turn on, it also drives and leads a relaxed hand.

        CalculationAssumption: tyre friction 0.6–1.2

        3Tail the end-cap you can take off

        +6–18 % less shake, on top of the inner pen (Rev J.1)

        In Rev J.1's lighter end-cap, coils push a 17.3 g tungsten weight up to ±4 mm sideways, and the push steadies the pen. It can also give short cues in pauses. It cannot steer letters. It weighs 29.6 g and replaces the rear cap. A plain weight of the same mass, locked in place, did better for one of three grips, so the moving weight stays optional until it beats a locked one. The 3-D model shows the first, 43.3 g design (a further 8–20 %).

        • Your thumb, index and middle finger hold the soft sleeve. It does not move.
        • A C-shaped ring at the front rests on the paper and carries your writing pressure. It is open on top, so you can see the ink.
        • The inner pen (the refill in its carrier, and the arm) hangs on a spring-steel pivot 76.5 mm behind the ball. A drum at the pivot sets the ink force and lifts the ball 0.5 mm between strokes, so strokes do not join. Proposed design
        • Coils push a cap of magnets just behind the pivot. The ball moves 6.6 times as far as the magnets. Calculation
        • The standard refill slides up to 26.6 mm in its carrier, so the ball stays on the paper at every tilt.
        • Two tiny motors under the battery turn two thin shafts that run to the heel: one steers the wheel, the other drives it. Proposed design
        • A motion sensor feels the shake. A page sensor beside the wheel sees the paper 1000 times a second. Two magnetic sensors read where the inner pen is.
        • The end-cap screws on in place of the rear cap. Its coils push the tungsten weight sideways against the shake.

        What the AI does
        • A tremor-line detector watches the page sensor and switches the listening tracker on only while a shake is present. It never switched on for tremor-free writing in the tests. Simulation · model HW1
        • A learned estimator runs in shadow mode: it logs its estimate beside the tracker until it passes on real recordings.
        • The app's clean copy, spelling help, and word completion.
        • Letter shapes in your own style for autowrite: 96 % legible from one to three of your letters, on synthetic writers. Calculation
        What the AI does not do, because it did not help
        • Guess your next letter and steer the tip toward it: it closed 0–1 % of the gap. Simulation
        • Let the ink trail the hand to smooth it: −4 % at 25 ms, and longer delays lose stroke ends. Simulation · model HW1
        • Let reinforcement learning drive the inner pen: it read more words but moved clean writing too much. It is used offline, to design how the pen shares control. Simulation · model HW1

          How you hold a pen, and where the shake comes fromThe tripod grip, why the fingers hold a part that stays still, and why shake and writing are hard to tell apart.

          The pen has to work with the way people already write. That is why the fingers hold a part that stays still.

          Most people hold a pen with three fingers: the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger. This is the “tripod” grip. The pen rests in the web between the thumb and the index finger.

          The fingers make the small up-down strokes and loops. The wrist makes strokes and the slant. The arm moves the hand along the line.

          A shake from the wrist and forearm moves the hand, the fingers and the whole pen together. So the pen cannot rely on your fingers staying still. It moves its own tip, and it uses the paper under the heel when it needs to push.

          Why the fingers hold a sleeve that stays still

          If the fingers held the moving part, they would feel every correction and fight it. With a fixed sleeve, the inner pen is inside, out of the way. The ring at the front carries your writing pressure, so the inner pen only has to steer.

          The inner pen swings on a pivot 76.5 mm behind the ball; the magnets sit just behind the pivot, so a small push there swings the tip 6.6 times as far. Proposed design Tablet studies of essential tremor found a typical shake of about 2 mm from side to side in spiral drawing. Literature · PDT-12

          Handwriting movements are mostly slower than 5 per second. Literature · CON-24
          Essential tremor: 4 to 12 per second. Literature · PDT-07, PDT-31
          Parkinson's rest tremor: 4 to 6 per second. It usually fades while writing. Literature · PDT-07, PDT-09

          The average essential tremor is about 6 per second, and it gets slower with age. Literature · PDT-31 That puts much of it in the overlap. Rev H's tracker did almost nothing at 6 per second (818 µm against 822 µm with an ordinary pen). Rev J's gated tracker listens harder while a shake is present and brings it to 540 µm. Simulation · model HW1

          This same split of speeds is why the tail weight can calm a shake but cannot write: its push is strong only at tremor speeds.

          Limits, and what must be tested firstWhat it cannot do, the first things to build and test, the problems that putting it all in one pen raised, and how far to trust the simulations.

          The design is a proposal. Putting the three parts into one 24 mm pen raised new problems, listed below.

          What it cannot do

          • The current inner pen cannot hold the ball on the paper for long: the coils would overheat within about a minute (see Known problems). A load-balanced nib is being designed. Calculation Simulation · sim2
          • It cannot write by inertia. No weight or gyroscope that fits a pen can move a letter. Calculation Simulation · model H1
          • It cannot write against you: a writer who resists always wins. Simulation · model HW1-D
          • It never writes text you did not choose.
          • Autowrite letters are small: 2.5 mm high by default, 3 mm possible. Bigger letters need more travel than a 24 mm handle can give within its heat limit. Simulation · model HW1 Calculation
          • With a strong shake the paper still shows shaky ink. The app's clean copy is digital and labelled as a copy.
          • It cannot perfectly tell shake from writing when both have the same rhythm, about 4–6 per second. Literature · CON-24, PDT-31
          • It does not cure tremor or small writing. It helps while you write, and it helps you practise.

          What to build and test first

          1. A load-balanced nib (study B) that removes the static load before another actuator is chosen; then the bench tests of the independent review's gates G1–G5 (study M). Proposed design · review response §3–§4
          2. Magnet test pieces, to measure the force on the inner pen (EXP-N01). A newer model gives 0.19 N/√W for Rev H, not 0.47. Calculation
          3. The wheel's grip on six kinds of paper (EXP-D01), and the heel on a bench with its force limit and release (EXP-D04, D07).
          4. Recordings of real writing with tremor (EXP-H01); then the tremor detector and the gated tracker offline (EXP-L01, L02), and the learned estimator (EXP-L04).
          5. How people split their grip on the 24 mm handle, with and without the end-cap (EXP-I01, K08), and the end-cap against the same weight locked in place (EXP-J16, gate G5). This decides whether the moving weight beats a plain weight.
          6. Spring fatigue and the pen lift (EXP-N05, N06).
          7. People: guidance and lead-through (EXP-D08), autowrite (EXP-N09), the end-cap and its cues (EXP-K03, K05). Whether people accept the pen is the question no simulation answers.

          Problems the integration found

          Holding the ball on the paper

          The refill spring presses the ball along the tilted pen, and the paper pushes back partly sideways. The inner pen's short magnet arm turns that into 1.6 W of coil heat at 50° and 4.7 W at 35°, all the time the ball touches the paper. It was left out of the battery and heat budgets below. See Known problems.

          CalculationSimulation · sim2

          Battery suspended (see known problems)

          The page sensor and the electronics take 115–165 mW before anything moves. With 1 mm of shake the pen lasts 6.1–7.3 h (7.8–8.2 h if the page sensor is switched off while only the shake is corrected); lead-through 5.9–6.9 h; autowrite 5.3–6.1 h; with the end-cap 4.3–6.6 h. The target is 8 h.

          Calculation

          Weight with the end-cap

          129.2 g against a 120 g limit, so the end-cap stays a module you can take off. The base pen is 87.0 g. Rev J.1, with its lighter 29.6 g end-cap, weighs 112.7 g.

          Calculation

          Load on the pivot

          The inner pen's magnets pull the pivot along the pen with up to 16.5 N, above the 14.5 N at which its thin strips buckle. The strips must work in tension, or a thrust pivot is added. Rev J.1 found that tension does not work and keeps thicker strips in compression instead (buckling at 55 N; a calculation only).

          Calculation: an upper bound

          Warmth suspended (see known problems)

          With 1 mm of shake, the skin over the coils reaches 38.7 °C in a 23 °C room and 45.7 °C in a 30 °C room. A 0.5 mm aluminium spreader brings the hot room down to 37.3 °C.

          Calculation

          Seeing the ink

          For an assumed eye position, the pen's own front hides the ink near the ball at 50–75° of tilt, as it already did in Rev H. Opening the top of the sleeve helps at 35°. People move their heads, so this needs a test with writers (EXP-J08).

          CalculationAssumption: eye position

          Magnets near the wheel motors

          The magnet cap on the inner pen (the nose's magnet cap) could put a detent of up to 0.16 N on the heel wheel through the rotor magnets of the heel motors (a free-space upper bound). A soft-iron cup may be needed (EXP-J02).

          Calculation

          Page sensor and pen roll

          The page sensor's lens stays in its working band only while the pen is rolled less than about 1.5°. This limits autowrite and slip detection until a lens with more depth is found (EXP-J04).

          Calculation

          Magnetic field outside the pen

          The field falls to 1 mT at about 23 mm from the pen, mostly from the heel motors. That level is an assumption for implanted devices until ISO 14117 is read; check before any trial (EXP-J07).

          CalculationAssumption: the limit

          Prior art

          A pen with an actuated nib that writes stored characters with a lift is patented (PAT-01). A freedom-to-operate review is needed before building autowrite with a pen lift.

          Literature · PAT-01
          How far to trust the simulations

          An independent review (29 September 2026) checked the design and the simulations. It confirmed the static load on the inner pen and the heel wheel's effect on clean writing (see Known problems). Its response and the claims register are in docs/reviews/ and docs/claims_register.md.

          The newer simulator (MuJoCo, “sim2”) was checked against the earlier hand–pen model on 56 test cases: the uncorrected ink error agreed within 3.1 % in all 56, the perfect-knowledge ratio within ±0.03 in 52 of 56, and the tracker's ratio within ±0.05 in 50 of 56 (the misses come from the Rev H tracker's frequency lock, not the physics). Simulation · sim2

          The synthetic writers write at about half adult speed and have about ten times the measured fast (8–12 per second) movement, which makes shake look harder to separate than it probably is. Until bench tests calibrate the simulator and a validation test passes, its results rank designs; they are not evidence of benefit. Literature · CON-20, CON-25
