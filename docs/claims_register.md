# Claims register: every headline claim and its current status

**Updated 2026-09-29 by the lead** (after the whole-pen physics study, sim2j, and the real-data study R). This is the one place to check before quoting a number. Nothing in this programme has been built or measured. Every claim below is a calculation (CALC), a simulation (SIM), literature (LIT) or an assumption (ASSUMPTION).

**Status words**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CURRENT | The latest calculation or simulation stands. It is not evidence of benefit to real people |
| SUSPENDED | A known error affects it. Do not quote it until the named study redoes it |
| SUPERSEDED | A later number replaces it (given) |
| CONTESTED | A later, more detailed simulation disagrees; quote both or neither |
| UNPROVEN | It rests on an assumption that nobody has measured |

**Which simulation to trust most.** The physics simulator sim2 with the Rev J pen (study sim2j, `docs/revJ_simulation.md`) models the pen's contact, stick-slip, masses and sensors in 3-D. The earlier hand–pen models (H1, HW1, HW1-D) are simpler. Where they disagree, sim2j is the better guide, but it still uses synthetic writers and synthetic tremor, and it ranks designs only until bench tests calibrate it (EXP-V01, V02, V04) and EXP-V05 validates it.

**What real recorded data changed (study R, `docs/real_data.md`, DEC-054, DEC-055).** Study R re-ran the trackers in the hand–pen model HW1 with real handwriting (UNIPEN, 14 adults on paper), real tremor (Parkinson's recorded at the pen tip; essential tremor recorded at the hand and scaled) and a measured-error page sensor, on held-out writers, texts and patients.
- **Real tremor at the tip is smaller and more irregular than assumed:** mild 0.03–0.16 mm, moderate 0.16–0.51 mm, severe above 0.51 mm (typically 1.7 mm). The plan had assumed 5–10 mm for severe.
- **No tracker built so far helps on real tremor.** At the severe size, readable words out of 10: ordinary pen 0.5, Rev J gated tracker 0.4, TCN 0.2. With perfect knowledge of the tremor, the same Rev J nose gives 7.0 (no tremor: 6.8). The nose has the reach; estimating the tremor in time is the limit.
- **Study E then searched for a tracker that works on real tremor, and found none yet** (`docs/real_tracker.md`, DEC-060, DEC-061): the best one, chosen and frozen on tuning data, took about a third off the severe tip tremor, but words read stayed at 0.5 of 10, and it disturbed one writer's clean writing by 0.49 mm. A network trained on real recordings looked better afterwards but must be tested again on new data (EXP-E10).
- **So every tremor result below that used synthetic tremor is marked "synthetic only".** None is a claim about real tremor until a tracker passes DEC-055's line on real inputs.

**The big correction (2026-09-29).** The moving nose's coils must hold the ball against a static sideways push from the paper. The refill spring presses the ball along the tilted pen, and the paper pushes back partly sideways. Study N's duty model left this out.
- **Rev J (C1S nose, 11.5 mm magnet arm):** 4.7 / 1.6 / 0.17 W at 35 / 50 / 75° pen angle (CALC; the independent review's figure, reproduced exactly).
- **In the physics simulation** the nose drew 2.25 W in tremor-free writing: about 1.1 W for this static load, 0.74 W for the servo reacting to its unfiltered position sensor (mostly a model artefact), and 0.4 W for friction and holding (SIM, `docs/revJ_simulation.md` §8.1).
- **Rev H (34 mm arm):** 0.36 / 0.13 / 0.01 W with its image-method magnet strength, or about six times that if the lower estimate holds (CALC).
- **Decision DEC-046:** the static load must be carried mechanically (≤ 0.1 W of coil heat), not by coil current. Study B chooses how. Every battery and heat claim below that includes the nose is **SUSPENDED** until then. See `docs/reviews/2026-09-29_review_response.md`.
- **The fix is designed (DEC-050, study B, 2026-09-30; CALC and SIM, nothing built).** A balanced translation nib (B1): the refill slides ±1 mm on four thin wires, and a contact-driven counter-face at the refill's rear end makes the spring's push and the paper's push parallel and opposite, so nothing is left across the pen at any angle. Holding heat at most 1.6 mW (limit 100 mW); continuous power 7.6 mW; about 38 h per charge (CALC; 33.5 h in simulation). It gives up reach: ±1.06 mm instead of ±6 mm, so autowrite has no hardware in the next prototype. The pen with this nib is called Rev K (proposed).
